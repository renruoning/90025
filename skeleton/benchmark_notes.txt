1. Task1 (parallel_split_words + word-frequency counting V2)
===========================================================

Design:
- split_words parallelized in two passes:
  (a) #pragma omp parallel for over every byte, independently replacing
      separators with '\0' (no cross-iteration dependency).
  (b) each thread scans its own contiguous byte range for word starts into
      a thread-local vector; results concatenated in thread order to
      preserve left-to-right ordering.
- Word counting: each thread accumulates into its own
  unordered_map<string_view, size_t> over a static split of the word list,
  then a sequential merge folds all local maps into one. No global sort
  (Ed clarification: Task1's word_counts/char_splits order is unspecified;
  only Task2's output.txt must be sorted/byte-exact).

Correctness:
- 52/52 unit tests pass (make test) after updating test_pipeline.cpp's
  order-dependent assertion (see git history).
- make smoke on Spartan: both 10M.txt and 100M.txt passed (md5 matched the
  locked baseline) — confirms output.txt byte-exact on real data, not just
  the toy fixtures.

2. Task2 (parallel_task2 — word-bucketed round processing)
===========================================================

Design (see src/parallel_task2.cpp):
- Round-to-round (which pair to merge next) stays strictly sequential —
  inherent to the greedy algorithm, cannot be parallelized without changing
  results.
- Within one round, if positions.size() >= kParallelThreshold (2000) and
  more than 1 OpenMP thread is available:
    Phase 1 (parallel, bucketed by word via unordered_map<word, vector<pos>>):
      each thread processes its assigned words' occurrences in original
      order, doing ONLY the word-local linked-list surgery (token/next/
      previous/alive arrays — safe because each word owns a disjoint index
      range) and records a MergeEvent per successful merge (capturing
      left_token/after_token AT the time of mutation, not re-derived later).
    Phase 2 (sequential replay): every MergeEvent is replayed through the
      *unmodified* remove_edge/add_edge/get_left_pair_state/
      get_right_pair_state helpers (same functions task2.cpp's sequential
      path uses), applying the cross-word-shared pair_state/group
      bookkeeping that can't safely be done in parallel.
  Below the threshold, falls back to process_positions_sequential (byte-
  identical to the reference inner loop) to avoid thread overhead on small
  rounds.

Bug found & fixed during verification (2026-09-12):
  The parallel structural phase originally cleared
  state.edge_group[right_position] = no_position immediately (copied
  verbatim from the sequential reference's ordering). But that field is
  read by the DEFERRED remove_edge(state, right_position, ...) call in the
  sequential replay phase, which needs it to find which pair_state's count
  to decrement. Clearing it early destroys that information before replay
  can use it, triggering the internal std::abort() safety check ("group ==
  no_position"). Fix: leave edge_group[right_position] untouched in the
  parallel phase; remove_edge clears it itself as its last step when
  called, and it is only cleared manually in the replay phase for the case
  where after_position is not live (so remove_edge is never called on it).

Correctness verification (tests/test_parallel_task2_diff.cpp):
  Differential test comparing task2() (sequential reference) against
  parallel_task2() bit-for-bit on the same CharSplit input, across:
    - all edge cases from test_task2.cpp (empty corpus, single word,
      HuggingFace example, tie-break, overlapping occurrences, multi-round,
      etc.)
    - a synthetic 5000-distinct-word corpus ("ab0".."ab4999") to force the
      very first round through the parallel path
    - a synthetic 3000-word corpus with internal pair overlap ("aaaa0" etc.)
      to stress the same-word overlapping-merge logic under the parallel
      dispatch
    - two corpora built from real split_words+task1 output (see section 3)
  Result: PASS on all cases, re-run 3x each at OMP_NUM_THREADS = 1, 2, 3, 4,
  8, 16 with no flakiness observed.

  On Spartan, 10M.txt output md5 with the real parallel_task2 (not the
  stub) also matches the earlier stub-recorded baseline
  (437efd507a4ce4ca82956f062fa9c2b2) — independent confirmation on a large
  real corpus, not just synthetic/unit-test fixtures.

  100M.txt output md5 (3581e913ac4c3f85000e8f8c933c8037) also matches the
  locked baseline at both 1 and 8 threads (2026-09-12) — correctness holds
  at the larger scale too.

Performance — WARNING, not a clean win yet:

  (a) Synthetic worst case, 150,007 distinct words ("wordN" + a handful of
      common filler words), local WSL machine:
        threads | task2 time
        --------|------------
          1      |  277-280 ms
          2      |  539-612 ms
          4      |  501-642 ms
          8      |  508-677 ms
      SLOWER with more threads. Root cause: the sequential replay phase's
      work is roughly comparable in size to the parallelizable structural
      phase, so Amdahl's law caps the win, and on this corpus (near-zero
      shared structure between words) the parallel phase barely helps while
      still paying bucketing/event-storage overhead.
      Two mitigations applied (both real improvements, neither fixed the
      regression on this specific corpus):
        - bucket positions by word using unordered_map instead of a vector
          sized to the whole vocabulary (the original version allocated a
          150,000-entry vector of vectors on every large round — dominant
          cost before the fix)
        - schedule(dynamic, 64) instead of schedule(dynamic) + events.reserve()
          per thread, to cut per-iteration scheduling overhead when many
          words contribute just one occurrence each

  (b) Real 10M.txt on Spartan, 126,860 distinct words (much more realistic
      pair-frequency distribution than (a)'s synthetic corpus):
        threads | task2 time | speedup
        --------|------------|--------
          1      |  597 ms    | 1.00x
          8      |  444 ms    | 1.34x
      A real, if modest, win — likely because real text has a much more
      skewed (Zipfian) pair-frequency distribution than the synthetic
      "wordN" corpus, so the big early rounds amortize the parallel phase
      better. Per the subject's speed-grading curve (H1 ~ O(p/log p) > H2 ~
      O(sqrt n) > H3 some speedup > P barely faster than baseline), 1.34x @
      8 threads is probably only H3-tier, not competitive yet.

  (c) Real 100M.txt on Spartan, 702,425 distinct words (2026-09-12):
        threads | task2 time | speedup
        --------|------------|--------
          1      |  3267 ms   | 1.00x
          8      |  3651 ms   | 0.90x  (SLOWER)
      Confirms (a) was not an artifact of the synthetic corpus: the
      regression gets WORSE as the corpus (and vocabulary) grows, not
      better. task1 still wins nicely at this scale (656->341ms split
      words = 1.92x, 2885->1746ms task1 = 1.65x), so total pipeline time is
      still down 6152->5398ms (1.14x), but task2 in isolation is a genuine
      regression at this scale and would very likely score 0 on the
      speed rubric ("slower than sequential baseline") as currently
      implemented. This makes the token_count/group_count optimization
      below no longer optional — it's required before submission, not a
      nice-to-have.
      md5 (3581e913ac4c3f85000e8f8c933c8037) matched the locked baseline at
      both thread counts — correctness still solid, only speed is the
      problem.

Optimization attempt #1 — token_count via OpenMP reduction (landed, commit
e95e29d): token_count[left]/[right]/[merged] only ever move by "frequency"
per successful merge for the same three token ids across a whole round, so
it's a plain sum — accumulated as an OpenMP `reduction(+:total_frequency)`
in the parallel structural pass instead of touching state.token_count once
per event in the sequential replay. Correct (61/61 tests, stress-tested
1-16 threads). Effect on the 150k-word local synthetic corpus was small
(~5-10%, e.g. 8-thread task2 508ms -> 500ms) — expected, since token_count
was never the dominant cost.

Optimization attempt #2 — group_count via resolve/apply split (TRIED AND
REVERTED, 2026-09-12, not committed): the plan was to split remove_edge
into resolve_removal() (decrement group_count, safe in parallel because an
existing group index is exclusively owned by one (pair_state, word) pair)
and apply_removal() (the cross-word-shared pair.count/word_count/free_head
tail, kept in the sequential replay). Implemented it, ran the differential
test suite immediately — ParallelTask2Diff.ManyWordsWithInternalOverlap
(the "aaaa0".."aaaa6" chained-overlap corpus) aborted.
  Root cause: resolve_removal clears edge_group[start] immediately, but the
  matching add_edge call that's supposed to RE-populate edge_group for the
  merged position is still deferred to the sequential replay phase (add_edge
  wasn't touched by this change). Within one word, when the SAME round has
  multiple chained/overlapping occurrences (event N's left_position is the
  exact position event N-1 just merged), event N's resolve_removal reads
  edge_group at a position whose edge hasn't been re-established yet — the
  sequential reference works because remove_edge AND add_edge happen
  together per occurrence, in order, before moving to the next occurrence;
  splitting them across "parallel now, sequential later" breaks that
  invariant for exactly the same class of bug as the original
  edge_group[right_position] timing bug (see section above), just
  reappearing in a different spot once add_edge and remove_edge are no
  longer both fully deferred together.
  Reverted cleanly (git checkout --) back to the token_count-only version.
  Real fix would require ALSO moving add_edge's group resolution into the
  parallel phase so remove+add for one occurrence stay interleaved like the
  reference — but add_edge's get_word_group relies on a single shared
  pair.last_word/pair.last_group cache slot per pair_state, which breaks
  under concurrent access from different words wanting groups for the same
  pair_state at the same time. Doing this properly needs a per-word-local
  cache replacing that single-slot cache (and still deferring genuine vector
  growth — new pair_states/groups — to a controlled point), which is a much
  bigger restructuring than "promote a decrement", not a small increment.
  Left as a bigger future task, not attempted again this session.

Optimization attempt #3 — raise kParallelThreshold from 2000 to 20000
(landed, commit pending, 2026-09-12): tuning-only change, zero correctness
risk (doesn't touch program semantics, only the round-size cutoff for
"go parallel vs fall back to sequential"). Tried schedule(static) instead
of schedule(dynamic, 64) first -- no real difference (~550ms @8 threads on
the 150k-word corpus, same as dynamic). Then swept kParallelThreshold:

  threshold | task2 @1 thread | task2 @8 threads | ratio
  ----------|-----------------|-------------------|------
  2000      | ~280 ms          | ~500-677 ms        | ~1.8-2.4x slower
  20000     | ~271-289 ms      | ~337-353 ms        | ~1.2x slower
  60000     | ~280-300 ms      | ~348-414 ms         | ~1.2-1.4x slower (noisier, no better than 20000)

  20000 and 60000 land in the same ballpark; picked 20000 (smaller, so it
  still exercises the parallel path on inputs that don't have 60000+
  occurrences in a single round -- matters for demonstrating genuine
  parallelism per R7.2, not just for raw speed).

  This is real progress -- cuts the regression roughly in half -- but is
  NOT a fix, just damage control: task2 is still measurably slower than
  sequential on the 150k-word local corpus. Confirms the earlier finding
  that mid-sized rounds (a few thousand to ~20000 positions) are where the
  parallel path loses the most; only genuinely huge rounds come close to
  breaking even locally. Real Spartan numbers with this threshold are
  still needed (couldn't test -- off campus, no VPN) before trusting this
  generalizes to 10M.txt/100M.txt.
  Also bumped up the two synthetic ParallelTask2Diff corpora
  (LargeDistinctWordsForcesParallelRound 5000->25000 words,
  ManyWordsWithInternalOverlap 3000->8000 words) so they still clear the
  new, higher threshold and keep exercising process_positions_parallel
  instead of silently falling back to sequential.


Optimization attempt #4 (v2) -- full rewrite: hash-map registry + critical
section, interleaved remove+add (src/parallel_task2_v2.cpp, kept as a
separate experimental file, NOT wired into the real parallel_task2 entry
point, 2026-09-12): dropped the group/free-list system entirely. Each pair
is a direct unordered_map<u64,PairRecord> entry; PairRecord carries its own
unordered_map<word,live_edge_count>, so no group indices/free list at all.
remove+add are kept interleaved per occurrence (fixes the attempt #2 bug by
construction -- they're never split across the parallel/sequential
boundary), with the shared registry protected by a single
`#pragma omp critical(pair_registry)` around each of the two bookkeeping
blocks; the structural linked-list surgery stays lock-free and parallel,
same as v1.

Correctness: verified via a parallel differential test suite
(tests/test_parallel_task2_v2_diff.cpp, mirrors the v1 one) -- all cases
pass, including 5 repeats at 16 threads with no flakiness. The design is
correct.

Performance: WORSE than v1 at every thread count tested, including
single-threaded (150k-word local corpus, task2_v2 standalone driver):
  threads | task2_v2
  --------|----------
  1       | 1291 ms   (v1 same corpus: ~280 ms -- ~4.6x slower at 1 thread)
  2       | 1425 ms
  4       | 1528 ms
  8       | 1668 ms   (gets WORSE with more threads, confirming lock
                        contention is real, not just theoretical)
Two independent causes stacked: (a) the nested unordered_map word_edges
design has real per-operation overhead (hash + allocation) compared to
v1's O(1) array-indexed group/free-list, so even the *sequential* baseline
is much slower; (b) the critical section is entered on essentially every
remove/add call (not just rare ones), so lock overhead scales with thread
count rather than being amortized away. Net conclusion: locking-based
designs are very unlikely to beat "defer everything, replay lock-free and
single-threaded" (v1's strategy) for this problem -- the lock's fixed cost
plus contention outweighs any parallelism gained, and switching away from
array-indexed bookkeeping to hash maps made the constant-factor problem
worse on top of that. Not pursuing further locking-based variants.

Optimization attempt #5 (v3) -- batch the round's "own pair" removal
(src/parallel_task2_v3.cpp, separate experimental file, NOT wired into the
real parallel_task2 entry point; based on v1's array/group design, not v2's
hash-map one, 2026-09-12): every occurrence pays a
remove_edge(state, position, frequency) to remove the CURRENT round's own
pair (left_token,right_token). By R5.4 (overlapping occurrences merge
left-to-right, exhaustively), every word that merges at least once this
round necessarily drains ALL its live edges of that pair -- so instead of
discovering that per occurrence, aggregate total_frequency and
"distinct words that merged >=1 time" (both via OpenMP reduction, same
trick as the token_count optimization) and apply ONE update to the
pair_state, recycling each word's group (captured once, from its first
successful occurrence) directly instead of through remove_edge.

Bug found and fixed during verification: the batching assumed a word's
group for its own pair is ONLY ever touched by remove_edge(position) calls
(the ones being batched away). That's false for a SELF-PAIR round
(left_token == right_token, e.g. merging (a,a) inside "aaaa"): an
overlapping occurrence's right_position removal can target the exact same
group, because right_position's forward pair (right_token, after_token)
coincides with (left_token,right_token) exactly when right_token ==
left_token. ParallelTask2V3Diff.ManyWordsWithInternalOverlap caught this
immediately (abort: pair.count underflow) -- diagnosed via targeted
fprintf instrumentation at each remove_edge abort site (not just guessing).
Fix: detect self-pair rounds (left_token==right_token) and bypass the
batching entirely for those, falling back to the same per-occurrence
remove_edge(position) the non-batched design uses (MergeEvent gained a
needs_own_removal flag). All other (non-self-pair) rounds still use the
batched path. Re-tested: 74/74 tests pass, stable across 1-16 threads and
5 repeats at 16 threads.

Performance: modest and scenario-dependent, NOT a clear win the way the
token_count optimization was.
  - On the 150k-word corpus (mostly 1 occurrence per word, the common
    case): v3 is roughly on par with v1, if anything slightly worse (both
    measured with the same standalone driver, interleaved, back-to-back --
    v1 avg ~214ms @8 threads, v3 avg ~225ms @8 threads over 5 runs). Makes
    sense: when words rarely contribute more than one occurrence, there's
    little to batch, and the extra own_groups bookkeeping is pure overhead.
  - On a corpus designed for the case this optimization targets (30000
    distinct words, each a 20-char repeated run, so many occurrences
    cluster within few words per round): v3 was faster (57ms vs 59ms @1
    thread, ~93ms vs ~104ms @8 threads, roughly 10% at 8 threads) but
    still nowhere near a transformative win, and single-sample (not
    repeated enough to rule out noise given the WSL host's load appears to
    have drifted over this long session -- see the note below).
  IMPORTANT CAVEAT: earlier numbers reported for v1 in this session
  (~280ms @1 thread, ~500-677ms @8 threads on the 150k-word corpus) were
  measured hours before v3's numbers, and a fresh, same-instant v1
  re-measurement with the same driver came back at ~138-225ms across
  thread counts -- meaning the WSL host's background load changed
  significantly over the course of this session, and any comparison across
  measurements taken far apart in time is unreliable. Only the
  interleaved, same-sitting comparisons above (v1 vs v3 back-to-back) are
  trustworthy; anything compared against the much-earlier v1 numbers
  elsewhere in this log should be treated as directional only, not exact.
  This is itself worth writing up in the report's methodology/limitations
  discussion (measurement protocol matters, single-run numbers on a shared/
  noisy machine are not reliable -- exactly why Spartan's dedicated
  interactive nodes, with repeated runs, are what actually counts).
